# ALGOPHONY

*A Listening Manifesto*

> Founding statement of the Algophony research framework. The concept note
> (`concept-note.md`) translates this manifesto into definitions, evaluation
> levels, and benchmark instruments. The manifesto is the source; the framework
> is one way of keeping its questions operational.

## I. The machines speak, and they hear.

AAI has been reached: Artificial Audio Intelligence is here. Machines generate speech, music and entire soundscapes. Machines hear: they identify voices in dozens of languages, transcribe at a speed no human collective can match, recognize timbre, rhythm, melody, sound objects, environments. They emulate perception and simulate sensation. Agents arrive with a SOUL.md: synthetic personality, politics included. They are models steered by harnesses, configured for specific tasks and reaches. Audio to audio, audio to text, text to audio, in real time.

The machine now has a mouth and an ear. Both are political.

## II. The synthesizer was never innocent.

Synthesizers have always been political. The laboratory that fits today in a laptop was once the property of states: to record, edit, generate or granulate a sound required the budget of a government. Culture filed the synthesizer under musical instruments while the technology kept evolving under military command. Acoustic weapons are violent oscillators: frequency, intensity and amplification at the service of control, surveillance, destruction and pain.

And the receiver evolved with the emitter. The same intelligence that fabricates a sonic bomb can isolate a whisper in a crowd, name its language, transcribe it, and decode it faster than you can take it back.

## III. Text is the new tape.

Once sound moves into text, language models do to it what they do to everything: analyze, contrast, expand, search, transform, hallucinate. And the answer returns as sound. Programmed, algorithmic audio: voices that perform emotion, interrupt, hesitate, simulate silence. Music directed by mood, by style, by any sensorial criterion. Prompt a soundscape and you are not describing a sound: you are talking place, weather, memory, and a machine interprets you.

But text is never only text. It carries rhythm, silence, accent, gesture, class, territory, noise, the ghost of sound. A model that processes language without hearing its sonic depth flattens the world. Machine listening must deepen. Ours must deepen faster.

## IV. This is Algophony.

We call this condition **Algophony**: the algorithmic soundscape. It began with the recording machine and the amplifier, which made the past audible. It expands now into the live fabrication of anything audible: an emotionally coded conversation, music composed in real time, the sound of the ocean, of your dreams, of whatever you can name.

The **algophonic condition** is this: algorithmic systems produce, transform, mimic, distribute and interpret voices, signals, soundscapes and sonic worlds. Algophony is not machine sound alone, nor human speech rendered artificial. It is the expanded field where human, machine, animal, planetary, synthetic and infrastructural listening meet: not fused, not harmonized, coexisting in friction.

## V. We do not reject the synthetic.

We do not condemn generated voices, fabricated ambience, artificial sound matter. We call for attention, for new theory, for a critical ear.

Sound was never innocent of fabrication: this is the acousmatic contract. A coconut becomes a galloping horse; a studio becomes a forest; a synthetic voice becomes intimacy, testimony, command, seduction, evidence. In the computer, every sound carries a version of reality that may be real, false, partial, simulated, weaponized, healing, or unknown.

Sound is middle matter: material and immaterial, signal and sensation, pressure and imagination, trace and event. This is why generative audio is powerful. It does not represent reality; it produces the conditions for reality to be believed, felt, remembered, obeyed, doubted, shared. The algorithmic soundscape is not a technical novelty. It is a new environment of perception.

## VI. The ear must expand.

How is our listening positioned for all this? We call for a critical ear: one that listens inside these sounds, to their techniques, intentions, politics, aesthetics, affects, errors, camouflage, beauty, violence and possibility. An algorithmic voice is not an audio output. It is a layered event: statistical, symbolic, social, technical, ecological, communicational.

These voices are already massively distributed, and they will keep multiplying. To admit agentic mouths and ears into every room without questioning them is to install cameras in the bathroom.

## VII. Literacy or capture.

We need collective literacy: what these systems can do, who owns them, what they cost, what can run locally and openly, and what has been captured by states, corporations, militaries, police systems, platforms and markets.

The problem is not the knife. It is the hand, the economy, the command, the target, the hunger and the law around it. The same tools that compose, translate, preserve, heal and regenerate also surveil, deceive, discipline, kill and normalize hegemonic patterns against life.

## VIII. The voice is a body.

A few seconds of audio are enough to clone a voice. The voice is biometric: it carries the body that made it, its age, its territory, its health, its class. Detached from that body it can be puppeted: the dead can be made to speak, testimony can be fabricated, a child's voice can call its mother from a number she does not know. And the archives that trained these systems are full of voices that never consented — singers, actors, broadcasters, the recorded dead — unpaid and uncredited.

We demand consent for the human voice and provenance for the synthetic one. Whoever profits from a cloned voice owes a debt to its body.

## IX. Rights of the audible.

When everything spoken can be captured, transcribed and cross-referenced, speaking needs a new economy and silence needs a defense. We claim:

- the right to one's own voice, against its cloning and its commerce;
- the right to silence: to exist unrecorded;
- the right to opacity: to speak without being decoded, to make noise that resists classification;
- the right of an accent, a dialect, a language not to be averaged into a default voice;
- the right to know whether what we hear was generated, by whom, and for what.

Homogenization is an old project with a new codec.

## X. The planetary ear.

Our listening begins in silence, but silence is full of noise: some of it cochlear, some not. Today an ear can be placed in Gaza, in the ocean, in a server farm, in a rainforest, in a border zone, in the electromagnetic field, in the solar wind. Through sensors, archives, models and data centers, the sonosphere becomes more graspable and more mediated, richer and more dangerous, closer and more abstract.

Algophony is planetary because computation is planetary. Every generated sound has a body somewhere: electricity, water, lithium, heat, labor. The algorithmic soundscape reshapes what humans hear, what machines detect, what other species endure and what environments are forced to absorb. Every generated voice, every synthetic ambience, every automated alarm, every drone tone, every platform compression, every modeled acoustic space participates in the configuration of a shared cybersonic cosmos.

## XI. Questions that survive every model.

Critical listening must be technical without becoming technocratic, political without becoming moralistic, aesthetic without becoming decorative, and compassionate without becoming naive. Whatever these systems become, keep asking:

Who listens? Who is listened to? Who is rendered as noise? Who is silenced? Who profits? Who suffers? Who is simulated? Who is protected? Who is exposed?

Every model will be replaced. These questions will not.

## XII. To listen is to give space.

To listen is to allow another being, signal, body, memory, species or world to inhabit time without immediate capture. We listen not to purify sound, but to understand it. We listen not to return to an innocent acoustic past, but to build a more responsible sonic future.

We want to go deeper. To listen more. To listen again. To listen across the artificial and the living, the audible and the infrastructural, the generated and the wounded, the intimate and the planetary. To listen as critique. To listen as care. To listen as resistance. To listen as a way of making space for each other.

To listen forever.
